Biology / Computational epidemiology / AMR

Joint Multidrug-Resistance Information in Indian AMR Surveillance: Bounds from Marginal and Pairwise Susceptibility Data and a Temporal Validation

Marginal frequencies alone miss joint resistance: K. pneumoniae all-five nonsusceptible was 16.54% observed against 1.17% under independence.

PublishedGlobal Youth Science Journal 3(5), 2026Read paper (DOI)Read on GYSJ
Question
What information is lost when resistance is represented only by marginal frequencies?
Method
Historical Indian ATLAS surveillance records for Klebsiella pneumoniae and Escherichia coli (641 target-species records, 544 with complete results for five prespecified antibiotics). The observed all-five nonsusceptible frequency was compared with an independence estimate and with feasible ranges constrained first by the marginal frequencies alone, then by all drug-pair frequencies, followed by a temporal validation.
Areas
Antimicrobial resistance, AMR surveillance, joint probability, antimicrobial susceptibility testing, quantitative analysis.
Results
K. pneumoniae: observed all-five nonsusceptible state 16.54%, against 1.17% under independence; marginal-only range 0–22.06%, pair-informed range 14.34–19.12%. E. coli: 5.51% observed, 0.18% under independence, 0–6.99% marginal-only, 5.15–5.88% pair-informed. A later K. pneumoniae burden of 25/72 (34.7%) exceeded the early-period upper bound of 16.5%. Marginal frequencies alone do not identify higher-order joint resistance, pairwise information can narrow the feasible range, and temporal transportability is not guaranteed.
Limitations
Historical, non-nationally representative data. The study does not estimate current Indian prevalence, recommend treatment, or justify reduced clinical antimicrobial susceptibility testing.
